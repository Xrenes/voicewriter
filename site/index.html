Hold a key. Speak. It types for you.

A tiny Windows app that turns your voice into text — anywhere you can type. Hold Alt+C, say your sentence, release. The words appear in the field you were already in. No window, no clicking around.

How it works

  1. Install and forget it. VoiceWriter starts with your PC and sits in the tray — no window on screen.
  2. Click into any text field — an email, a chat box, your editor, a form.
  3. Hold Alt+C and speak. Release the keys when you're done.
  4. Your sentence is typed in — punctuation and spelling included — right where your cursor was.

Accurate by default

Uses Groq's Whisper Large v3 for state-of-the-art accuracy on names, numbers and punctuation. Falls back to a local model when you're offline.

Private

Local mode never leaves your machine. Your Groq API key is stored in the Windows Credential Manager, not a plain file.

Out of the way

No window unless you open it from the tray. ~25 MB of RAM. Click anywhere else and the settings panel tucks itself away.

Hold-to-talk

One motion: hold, speak, release. No toggling, no "start/stop recording" buttons. The hotkey is rebindable.

First launch on Windows

The app isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may show "Windows protected your PC." To continue: click More info → Run anyway. This is expected for small independent tools; the source of the installers is the GitHub Releases page.

After install, VoiceWriter runs in the background. Open the tray (the ▲ near the clock), click the VoiceWriter icon, and paste a free Groq API key from console.groq.com — or skip it and download the local model from the same panel.
