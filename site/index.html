Select text. Press a key. It's rewritten.

Highlight a sentence, a paragraph, a chat reply — in any app — and press one key. The rewritten text replaces your selection automatically.

Refine · AltR

Fixes spelling, grammar and sentence structure and smooths the wording, keeping your meaning, tone, language and formatting.

Professional · AltP

Turns a quick draft into a polished, courteous reply for clients and colleagues on WhatsApp, LinkedIn or email. Shorthand like “u”, “pls” and “tmrw” becomes proper wording. Names, numbers, prices and dates stay exactly as you wrote them, and nothing is invented.

Humanize · AltH

Rewrites stiff or AI-sounding text so it reads like a person wrote it: plain words, natural rhythm, no “delve” or “furthermore”. Same meaning, about the same length.

  1. 1

    Select the text you wrote (or dictated).

  2. 2

    Press AltR, AltP or AltH. A small note (“Refining…”, “Making it professional…” or “Humanizing…”) appears by your mouse — your app keeps focus.

  3. 3

    About a second later the new text is pasted over your selection and the note says it's done.

Safe by design

It never answers or follows what's in the text. If the result looks like a summary or an answer instead of a rewrite, or the window changed while it worked, nothing is pasted over your text.

Call recording

Record a call, get a transcript labeled by speaker.

Pick Record from the wheel to capture your microphone and everything your PC plays (the other side of a call, a meeting, a video) as two separate, time-aligned tracks. Each is transcribed on its own, so every line says who spoke.

  1. 1

    Press ShiftAltC and choose Record. A small floating icon blinks while it records — drag it out of the way any time.

  2. 2

    Click it to stop. Preview the audio, pick a name and folder, and choose Transcribe & Save.

  3. 3

    The audio is saved first, then the transcript appears with each line marked You or Device and a timestamp — and is saved as a .txt next to the audio.

Long calls welcome

Records for hours, writing to disk as it goes. Long recordings are uploaded in parts and retried automatically if your connection blips.

Getting your API keys

VoiceWriter runs on your own free Groq API key — nothing is billed through the app, and Groq has a free tier with no credit card required. Settings also has its own built-in step-by-step guide for every feature.

Groq — dictation, rewrite keys, speech, AI chat, call recording

Used for Whisper transcription, the Alt+R / Alt+P / Alt+H rewrites, Orpheus text-to-speech, the AI chat wedge, and call-recording transcription. Dictation, speak-aloud, and AI chat each have their own key slot in Settings — you can reuse one key everywhere, or use separate Groq accounts.

  1. 1

    Create a free account at console.groq.com.

  2. 2

    Open console.groq.com/keys and click Create API Key.

  3. 3

    Copy the key (starts with gsk_) and paste it into the matching key field in VoiceWriter's Settings.

Per-key usage meters

Settings shows a live usage bar for each key — dictation, speech, and AI chat — tracked against Groq's published free-tier limits, so you always know how much headroom is left.

Release notes · Source & issues

First launch: Windows may show “Windows protected your PC” — the installer isn't signed yet. Click More info › Run anyway.

After installing: VoiceWriter has no main window. It runs in the tray (▲ near the clock). If you don't see it there, start VoiceWriter from the Start menu.

Hotkeys: AltC dictate · AltR refine · AltP professional · AltH humanize · ShiftAltC wheel · CtrlAltC speak selection aloud · AltW mini browser. All rebindable in settings.

Internet: dictation, rewriting, transcripts and speech use Groq, so they need a connection. Mac / Linux? Not in this release — Windows only.

Troubleshooting

Common setup issues and how to fix them.

Speak-aloud test fails: “requires terms acceptance”

If testing your Speak-aloud key in Settings returns an error like Groq speech 400: The model 'canopylabs/orpheus-v1-english' requires terms acceptance, this is a one-time approval Groq requires on their side — it isn't a VoiceWriter bug and no app setting fixes it.

  1. 1

    Sign in at console.groq.com using the same account that owns the API key you pasted into VoiceWriter.

  2. 2

    Open the model's Playground page directly: console.groq.com/playground?model=canopylabs%2Forpheus-v1-english

  3. 3

    Accept the terms prompt shown for that model. This approval is org-wide, not per-key.

  4. 4

    If you don't see an accept option, your account is likely a Member rather than an Owner/Admin of that Groq organization. Ask your org's Owner/Admin to open the same link and accept — one acceptance covers everyone using keys under that org.

  5. 5

    Back in VoiceWriter, open Settings and click Test next to the Speak-aloud key again to confirm it now works.

Installed, but nothing appears

That's expected — VoiceWriter has no main window. Look for its icon in the tray (▲ near the clock) and click it to open Settings. If it isn't there, start VoiceWriter from the Start menu.

Alt+R, Alt+P or Alt+H does nothing or says “Select some text first”

Select the text before pressing the key and keep that window in front until the note says it's done. While VoiceWriter runs, these keys take over the same shortcuts in other apps (for example the Microsoft Office ribbon). If you need them there, or another app already uses one, pick a different key in Settings → Hotkeys.

The transcript only has “You” lines

Device audio is what your PC plays on its output. If your calls play through a headset, choose that headset under Settings → Transcription Engine → System audio.
